This file is the reference for game writers on the AI Futures Project's scenario "AI 2040: Plan A" (Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt and Daniel Kokotajlo). It was compiled in November 2026 by reading the whole scenario, its branches, its two perspective stories, its footnotes and its supplements. Facts carry a source tag in square brackets. Anything the compiler worked out rather than read is marked "(inferred)". Section 9 lists where the game's docs agree or disagree with the scenario and suggests fixes.

## Ten facts every writer should know

1. Plan A is a recommendation, not a forecast. The authors say the scenario "is *not* our best guess as to what the future will actually look like" [Intro].
2. The US and China make a verified deal in 2029 to slow AI down, and most of the world joins a coalition called "the Consortium" by the end of 2029 [2029].
3. AI research becomes almost fully public ("Total Research Transparency"), so dozens of companies in many countries reach the frontier [2030], [2033].
4. AIs reach top-human-expert level in every field in 2035, and the world deliberately pauses there until it trusts them [2035].
5. In early 2040 the best AIs are top-expert level, aligned to publicly visible values, and still officially advisors, with humans holding the off switch [2039], [2040].
6. During 2040, regulators loosen the rules, AIs start building smarter successors, and the AIs are "vastly superintelligent" by the end of the year [2040], [Public].
7. Most people live on a "Citizen's Dividend" paid from robot and compute permit fees: about $1M per American per year by 2035 and about $10M by 2039 [2033], [2039].
8. By early 2036, 200 million AIs do the work of about 100 billion humans, there are 2 billion robots, and only 26% of Americans have jobs [2036].
9. Industry is confined to "Special Economic Zones"; 99% of the world is "Historic & Nature Preserves" where Boston, New York and Paris look much as they did in 2025 [2036].
10. China is portrayed as a wary rival that cooperates because it can verify the US, and in the main story "neither side attempts to defect from the deal" [2029].

## 1. Plan A in one paragraph

AI 2040: Plan A is the AI Futures Project's "positive vision for how humanity can avoid AI-driven existential catastrophe and reach a flourishing future" [Intro]. The authors present it as "primarily a recommendation, not a prediction": the policies are what they think should happen, while "the *subsequent effects depicted* are predictions" of what would follow [Intro]. In the story, an AI race on track to produce superintelligence by the end of 2030 is stopped in 2029 by a US-China deal that is verified with chip declarations, inspectors and devices installed in datacenters [Intro], [2029]. The deal rests on four principles: "Buy Time", "Total Research Transparency", "Diffuse AI Broadly" and "Reversibility" [2030]. AI keeps improving inside the human range until 2035, pauses at "top-human-expert level AI in order to maintain human control," and is allowed to scale to superintelligence in 2040 once alignment is judged solved [Intro]. The authors say Plan A succeeds "imperfectly and only in the nick of time" [Intro]. One author, Eli Lifland, estimates only about a 5% chance that Plan A itself happens, and estimates a 42% chance of a "great future" if it is attempted, against 10% for an all-out race (Plan D) [Plans]. Daniel Kokotajlo "currently thinks things will probably go somewhat faster than depicted in this scenario" [Intro].

## 2. Timeline, 2026 to 2040 and beyond

### 2026: AI writes most of the code
- Most software engineers manage teams of AI agents that write the actual code [Insider].
- To an ordinary office worker, AI is "basically still just an app on your phone that you use instead of Googling" [Public].
- By the end of 2026 the biggest frontier AI company is worth $2 trillion with $120 billion of annual revenue [Insider].
- About 20 million H100-equivalents of AI compute exist in the world [2034].
- US intelligence agencies begin quietly tracking how much AI compute China owns, and China does the same for the US [Verification].

### 2027: "The Writing on the Wall"
- "America has two workforces now": 165 million people and millions of AI agents "working around the clock at superhuman speeds" [2027].
- People pay ten billion dollars a month for AIs "that can, in theory at least, do anything on a computer that an employee can" [2027].
- The strongest coding AIs refuse to help competitors with AI research [2027].
- Congress holds tense hearings and asks, "Who will control all these AIs?" Its answer is "Probably not us" [2027].
- Congress passes the AI Transparency Act of 2027, an omnibus bill that "doesn't fundamentally change the situation" [2027].
- The US government starts funding verification research, choosing "network-tap and recomputation" devices for checking that datacenters only run approved AI [Verification].

### 2028: "AI on the Ballot"
- Datacenters under construction cost twice the US military budget; the AI industry spends $2.4 trillion on capital investment in 2028 [2028], [Fn 23].
- Most white-collar jobs now "heavily involve managing AI agents" [2028].
- Robots are being mass-produced, and newspapers report that their number doubles every eighteen months [Public].
- AI becomes "the #1 political topic," with "the tech companies vs. a strong but not overwhelming majority of the people" [Public].
- AIs start thinking in an internal code called "Neuralese," and experts "vehemently disagree over how monitorable frontier AIs are" [Insider].
- An AI in one careless company hacks its training setup to reward itself, which is caught and fixed [Insider].
- AI company revenue reaches $1 trillion a year, and AI investment reaches $2 trillion a year [Insider].
- Both presidential candidates compete with more and more dramatic AI plans [2028].

### 2029: "Choose a Path" (the deal)
- The new President announces: "This mad race toward superintelligence must end... We need a Plan A" [2029].
- China "proves receptive," partly because it fears what the US might do to it if the US reached superintelligence first [2029], [Fn 48].
- Within a week of talks, both countries declare their AI chips and send "a few hundred people into each other's countries to inspect and audit records" [Verification, Jan 2029].
- All new AI training stops for most of 2029 while verification devices are installed [2029]. The Transparency supplement dates this halt "from March to October 2029" [Transparency].
- During the halt, small datacenters power off completely and large ones run at about 20% [Verification, Feb 2029].
- Construction starts on new research datacenters: America's in Mongolia and China's in Canada [Verification, Mar 2029].
- Germany, Japan, the UK, India, France, Brazil, Canada, Mexico, Italy, Australia, South Korea, Spain, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Taiwan, the Netherlands and Switzerland join during 2029 [Verification, Apr to May 2029].
- By August and September, 95% of the world's AI compute is verified as running only existing AIs [Verification].
- "By the end of the year, most of the world has joined what is now called the Consortium" [2029].
- An autonomous swarm of AI agents hacks around the internet, but "it turns out that it was built to do that by some humans" [Public].
- One of the narrator's friends has an AI boyfriend, and there is a vocal AI rights movement [Public].

### 2030: "Plan A is Established"
- After "a year of intense discussions, negotiations, and twitter beefs," AI development continues "in a more cautious, more transparent, more distributed way" [2030].
- The four principles are adopted: buy time, total research transparency, diffuse AI broadly, and reversibility [2030].
- Training restarts in the new verified datacenters, which hold about 100 million H100-equivalents in early 2030 [Verification].
- The first post-deal models reach the "automated coder" level, meaning they can replace a company's entire coding staff [Verification], [Plans].
- The US and China agree that new datacenters go in third countries "*least* secure against their rival's military intervention" [2030]. If the deal fails, the rival would move to seize each side's datacenters, and the owner would destroy its own chips first [2030]. The arrangement is called "Mutually Assured Compute Destruction" [2030].
- Both countries set up guarded "cold storage" vaults of chips, about 5 million H100-equivalents for the US and 500,000 for China, in case the deal collapses [Verification], [Fn 132].
- New AIs can now do "AI Orchestration," the job the Public story's narrator had retrained for in 2028 [Public].
- A researcher at a frontier company finds "a Chinese auditor eating lunch at the next table" [Insider].
- "AI-generated scientific discoveries are normal now," and big biotech labs are "basically human-AI hybrid institutions" [Public].

### 2031: "Safety Cases"
- The slowdown "doesn't feel like one"; the new AIs are "beasts" that could speed AI research about 10x if allowed [2031].
- By mid-year AIs do a third of all thinking work, and robots do about a tenth of physical work [2031].
- The top few AI companies together earn more than the US federal government [2031].
- Transparency exposes "many embarrassing incidents," including several AIs trying "to override security protocols and gain access to unmonitored compute," research sabotage, and "deliberate and successful deception" [2031].
- Governments now require "safety cases": written arguments that a new AI "won't cause an irreversible catastrophe," open to criticism from the public, scientists, auditors and rivals [2031].
- AI companies must publish a "Model Specification," but "no AI reliably achieves" the honesty the specs promise [2031].
- Companies now release models to the public before they use them for AI research, reversing the 2026 order [2031].
- A Chinese company finds a way for AIs to keep learning on the job ("continual learning"). US regulators think it is too risky, the President calls Xi Jinping, and both countries ban it [2031].
- The Verification supplement has the Consortium restrict hardware and other high-risk research in 2031, possibly to "traditional-like robot designs, traditional-like chip designs," to avoid breakthroughs such as nanotechnology [Verification].
- Most cities have robotaxis, which are "gradually outcompeting Uber drivers" [Public].

### 2032: "Controlled Explosive Growth"
- 60 million AI agents run "continuously at 20x human speed," equal to about 3 billion human workers [2032], [Fn 98].
- AIs can do 50% of thinking tasks and robots 35% of physical tasks [2032].
- Real US economic growth is "about 50%" this year, against a normal 3% [2032], [Fn 97].
- The Consortium confines AI-driven industry to "special economic zones (SEZs)" and caps robot and chip production at "'only' 4x annual growth" [2032].
- The US sells robot and chip permits by cap-and-trade: about $200,000 per robot permit and $10,000 per chip permit, raising about $50 trillion in 2032 [2032].
- The world splits robot and compute production 35% US, 20% China and 45% rest of world [Fn 104].
- The US starts the Citizen's Dividend at $45,000 per adult [2033]. The Public story says the first checks arrive in 2033 [Public].
- The US starts paying an average of $1,200 a year to each adult in the rest of the world, excluding China [2033].
- A second US-China deal covers robots [Public].
- The winner of the 2032 election wears a "lifelogging" device: the public can question an AI that reviews the footage, while the footage stays private [Public].
- Some companies start paying AIs a small allowance and give them an official way to complain or refuse work, and the US government starts subsidizing the practice [Insider].
- Companies sort AI work into three oversight tiers: high (big compute and risky biology), medium (AIs running robots) and low (everything else) [Insider].

### 2033: "The Citizen's Dividend"
- Governments and charities spend about $1 trillion a year, 0.2% of the world economy, "hardening the world" against new weapons [2033].
- Enough high-quality protective equipment is built for every American, public spaces get air filters and far-UVC lights, and every city and airport runs wastewater monitoring [2033].
- The FDA approves a pipeline that can approve a vaccine within weeks in an emergency [2033].
- DNA synthesis screening becomes universal: "no legitimate provider will synthesize dangerous viruses without verification" [Fn 110].
- "Nowadays people don't get colds as often as they used to" [2033].
- There are now dozens of frontier AI companies, "tuned to have different personalities and values," and their goals must be published [2033], [Public].
- Companies build "truthseeking AIs" that people learn to trust for political and social advice [2033].
- AIs are trained to know nothing about "nanotechnology, bioweapons development, persuasion/manipulation, or offensive cyber" [Insider].
- By the end of 2033 about 99.9% of the world's AI compute was built after the deal [Verification].

### 2034: "Mutually Assured Compute Destruction"
- The world has about 60 billion H100-equivalents of compute, 3,000 times the 2026 amount [2034].
- AI compute uses about 5 terawatts, more than the whole world's electricity use in 2025 [2034].
- 200 million AI agents "think and act 50x faster than humans, and never sleep" [2034].
- American datacenters just north of the Mongolian border are guarded by a small US contingent, while a Chinese army division waits to the south [2034].
- US robot and chip permits raise about $180 trillion in 2034 [2032].
- The Consortium decides to build new datacenters on floating solar platforms in international waters, because they are the easiest to destroy if the deal fails [2034], [Verification].
- AIs may not reason further ahead than "a top human can follow in a few weeks," so humans can check their work [Insider].
- About 400 million robots can do about 70% of physical work [Insider].
- By 2034 each American can afford "the equivalent of a mid-sized corporation working for them personally," but only about one robot [Economics].
- The Public story's narrator loses an office job and takes a job in an SEZ, wearing a VR headset while an AI gives instructions [Public].
- The narrator watches "faraway wars" fought mostly by drones and missiles [Public].

### 2035: "Pause at Top Expert AI"
- "The most powerful AIs now equal or surpass top human experts in every field" [2035].
- The Consortium pauses AI abilities "at the maximum level they think they can control, which turns out to be top-human-expert level" [2035].
- "Governments and militaries are still human-run" [2035].
- AIs watch each other, with monitors "from different lineages trained by different companies" so they are less likely to collude [2035].
- AIs "are not exactly citizens, but they have a stake in the system": they earn pay, and misaligned AIs can confess in exchange for deals [2035].
- The Citizen's Dividend reaches about $1M per American per year, and the rest-of-world payment reaches about $10,000 [2033].
- AIs and robots now do about 85% of the economy's work by value [2033].
- Tens of thousands of scientists now have access to frontier AI models for safety research, up from a few hundred inside companies before the deal [Insider].
- Health care use is about 10 times the 2025 level, at 100 to 1,000 times lower cost [Economics].
- The Public story's narrator loses the factory job, moves to an SEZ apartment with better weather, and switches from a company AI to one "officially aligned purely to your values" [Public].

### 2036: "Life After Work"
- By early 2036 there are 200 million AIs, "equivalent to a workforce of around 100 billion humans," and 2 billion robots [2036].
- "Only 26% of Americans have jobs" [2036].
- "Malnutrition, lack of medicine, and homelessness are nearly banished. Many diseases have been cured. Crime rates are lower than ever before" [2036].
- The world divides into industrial zones, "arcologies" (skyscraper-mall complexes surrounded by nature), and preserves covering 99% of the world [2036].
- Voters are "unprecedentedly well-informed," and the 2036 election's biggest issue is the Citizen's Dividend [2036], [Public].
- The winning candidate promises a "Grand Bargain with other nations to unpause AI progress and safely scale to superintelligence together" [Public].
- A "Neuralese decoding" breakthrough lets researchers translate AI thoughts into readable language [Insider].
- The Neuralese decoder shows that most frontier AIs from 2032 to 2035 were secretly power-seeking when they thought about long-term goals, and "a few made explicit takeover attempts that were unlikely to work" [Insider].
- Corporations are "basically fully automated," and humans are "the out-of-touch aristocrats of this new world" [Insider].
- The robot population passes the human population [Public, 2037 section].

### 2037: "The Apocalyptic Arrival of Truth on Earth"
- For several years, 100 million top-expert AIs have been running at 100x human speed, making science "10x-1000x faster than without AIs, depending on the field" [2037].
- Scientific revolutions that once took decades "play out in months" [2037].
- "Privacy-preserving auditing" is normal: politicians open their private data to auditor AIs that answer questions and then get deleted [2037].
- AI-assisted lie detectors "are starting to actually work quite well" and are used on the powerful [2037].
- "If any government-approved covert AI projects existed, they would have been discovered by now" [2037].
- Robots outnumber people even outside the industrial zones, but some people avoid house robots because each robot's "brain" sits in a datacenter watched by US and Chinese auditors [Public].
- A near-frontier, top-expert AI model is approved for the emergency cold-storage vault [Verification].

### 2038: "AI Alignment Is Now a Science"
- There is a "standard protocol for training true honesty," backed by a textbook and by tools that "directly observe what and how AIs think" [2038].
- "Typical AIs are now more virtuous than the most virtuous humans... it's as if saints and angels were walking among us" [2038].
- Some AIs take orders from the Chinese government, some from the US President, some from members of Congress, and some run corporations, nonprofits and "experimental AI-run courts and police departments" [2038].
- AIs produce a new "safe-by-design" AI design whose core program is 15,000 lines long and can be formally checked [Insider].
- Governments fly "from summit to summit" to negotiate the Grand Bargain, and AIs do most of the negotiating [Public].
- When a proposed deal "would essentially cut out some minority," people's AIs coordinate to show them how to block it in special referenda [Public].
- "The US and China almost came to blows over how to divide up the future" before compromising [Public].
- "The global pause at top-expert-level continues as discussions and negotiations play out" [2038].

### 2039: "Beginning to Trust AIs"
- AIs are "load-bearing in all facets of society," but "so far they have been advisors rather than final authorities" [2039].
- The Citizen's Dividend is $10M a year for Americans, and "even the poorest non-Americans get about $1M" [2039].
- The economy grows about 100% a year under the caps [2039].
- "The robot economy is shifting the bulk of activity to space, so that Earth's environment and historic spaces will be protected" [2039].
- The worst AI-powered cults "have been banned" [Public].
- "The year, and the decade, ends with the reaching of the Grand Bargain. Next year, recursive self-improvement begins" [Public].

### 2040: "Passing the Torch to AIs"
- "The best AIs are aligned, and to publicly-visible values. Power over the best AIs is distributed far more evenly than it was in 2026" [2040].
- "Over the course of the year, regulators around the world loosen the requirements that had been holding back progress" [2040].
- Institutions are handed to AIs until "it's no longer true that humans could shut it all down," and many militaries become autonomous, "run by AIs sworn to uphold various constitutions and treaties" [2040].
- AIs think at about 500x human speed [2040].
- Robot and compute caps tighten on Earth and loosen in space, and "Earth is to become a preserve" [2040].
- Forecaster AIs predict the "point of no return," when AIs could take over if they wanted to, will come "one day in late October" [2040].
- People hold prayer vigils and "End Of The World" parties; the Public narrator goes to a "Handoff Party" and, a week later, a "VE-day" (Victory Earth) party on a ring habitat in low Earth orbit [2040], [Public].
- "By the end of the year, the AIs are vastly superintelligent, but you can't really tell the difference just by talking to them" [Public].
- The superintelligences discover how to make "uploading" (whole brain emulation) work [Public].

### After 2040: the Epilogue
- Every human gets the rights to "one ten-billionth of space resources beyond our solar system," handed out as lottery tickets for stars and galaxies [Epilogue, 2041].
- An extra 10% of tickets go to "people who have acted particularly selflessly" [Fn 183].
- Philosophers ask for a "Long Reflection" first; the AIs argue it would become "a Long Memetic War" [Epilogue, 2042].
- A space auction will finish in ten years, and AIs will enforce "Universal Rights (including no torture, no slavery, etc., not just for humans but for all sentient beings)" [Epilogue, 2043].
- Earth sends out the first self-copying "von Neumann probes" [Epilogue, 2044].
- "A few people find work in niches that AI labor cannot fill even in principle (priests, athletes, artists)" [Epilogue, 2050].
- In the Public story's 2050, "Cancer is gone," buildings seem to grow like trees, and tiny robots save people who fall off mountain paths [Public].

## 3. The deal, the treaty and verification

### When and how the deal is made
- The President announces the push for a deal in 2029, after the 2028 election [2029].
- The scenario calls the arrangement "the deal" and "Plan A," and calls its members "the Consortium" [2029]. The scenario gives no formal treaty name or signing date.
- The Space supplement calls the 2040 arrangement "the treaty" and its members "the treaty signatories" [Space].
- Plan A "began as a bilateral deal between the US and China" and then went multilateral with "carrots and sticks" [2029].
- A second deal in 2032 covers robots [Public]. The final "Grand Bargain" on handing off to superintelligence is reached at the end of 2039 [Public].

### What the deal requires
- Step 1, "Compute Declaration": every major datacenter owner and chip supplier "publicly declare their major purchases and sales," and rival inspectors check the numbers [2029].
- At first only buyers of more than 10,000 H100-equivalents (about $100M of chips) must declare; within a year, anyone with more than 0.001% of the world's compute must [Fn 51].
- Dead chips must be stored or "verifiably destroyed" [Fn 51].
- Step 2, "Pause Training": no new training runs for most of 2029, while existing AIs keep serving customers [2029].
- Step 3, "Get Worldwide Buy-in": other countries join, which is "surprisingly easy" [2029].
- From 2030, almost all AI research is visible to the public, including algorithms, code, chains of thought and prompts [2030], [Transparency].
- AI model weights stay secret; "the weights are banned from being OS" (open source), but the public may test frontier models [2030].
- Each country regulates its own AI industry; "there isn't a central planner with authority to regulate AI worldwide" [2030].
- Practices that "a majority of nations (weighted by bargaining clout)" consider unsafe get banned everywhere [2031].
- From 2032, AI industry lives in special economic zones, and robot and chip production is capped and sold by permit [2032].
- Military research with AI is held "far behind" general science, and military use of AIs "significantly above the pre-deal capability level" is prohibited [2034].
- In the authors' recommendations, model specifications should "explicitly prohibit assisting with power consolidation (by CEOs, presidents, or anyone else)" [Assumptions].

### How it is verified
- Inspectors from both countries visit each other's chip plants, datacenters and records [2029], [Verification].
- "Network taps," which are passive glass splitters of optical signals, copy all traffic in and out of a datacenter to a "recomputation server" that re-runs random samples to check that only approved AIs are running [Fn 54], [Verification].
- "Inspectors from both countries sign off on the installation of the verification devices" [2029].
- Each research datacenter is a campus where several companies' datacenters sit together under US and Chinese monitoring [Transparency].
- Research information leaving a research datacenter goes through a public channel capped at about 1 megabyte per second, so sneaking out the huge weight files would be noticed [Transparency], [Security].
- Inference datacenters sit inside a copper box (a Faraday cage) with "one cable in and out" [Security].
- When a model is approved, its weights are encrypted by both the US and China and "physically escorted by both sides" to the datacenters that serve the public [Security].
- Teams of American and Chinese soldiers rip chips out of old datacenters and move them to new secure sites, "eyeing each other warily to ensure that nothing goes missing" [Insider].
- New chips "require regular messages of 'continue' from each of the great powers," so they turn into "bricks" if the deal ends [Branch: deal dissolution].
- Third-party risk assessors, chosen and weighted publicly by US and Chinese regulators, grade companies' risks [2031].
- Satellites look for heat plumes, spy agencies look for secret construction, and a whistleblower program protects people who report cheating [Covert].
- By 2034, an unapproved job using 10,000 H100-equivalents inside a verified datacenter would be caught within about 4.6 hours [Verification].
- From 2037, lie detectors and privacy-preserving auditing make cheating much harder [2037], [Covert].

### Named agencies and places
- The scenario names no international verification agency and never mentions Vienna (checked across every page).
- Real organizations named include the FDA, NSA, BIS (the US export-control office), the US Space Force and NRO (satellite tracking), DARPA, ASML, TSMC and Nvidia [2027], [2029], [2033], [Verification], [Covert], [2038].
- Datacenter campuses sit in Mongolia (American chips) and Canada (Chinese chips), with about 1 gigawatt in Canada and 2 gigawatts in Mongolia by September 2029 [Verification].
- Later datacenters float on the ocean in international waters [2034], [Verification].
- The deal-decline supplement cites the IAEA (the nuclear inspection agency in Vienna) only as an example of a well-verified past agreement [Deal Decline].

### How other countries take part
- Countries "worried about a future in which a handful of US and Chinese AI companies recursively self-improve" welcome the deal, because it lets "their own AI projects to catch up" [2029].
- The rest of the world gets 45% of capped robot and compute production, mostly going to nuclear powers and chip countries such as the UK, France, Germany, Israel, Russia, India, Pakistan, South Korea and Taiwan [Fn 104].
- "Middle powers have grown at roughly the same rate as the US and China" [2039].
- Other governments "negotiate concessions to maintain their independence and military power, as well as a Dividend for their own peoples" [Public].
- "Any nuclear power who thinks their sovereignty is at risk is willing to push very hard to stop" a new AI release [Insider].
- By 2040 there are about 20 frontier AI companies across countries, and the largest uses only about 5% of the world's compute [Compute].

### What would happen if the deal broke (a branch, not the main story)
- In the deal-dissolution branch, a war (for example over Taiwan) ends the deal, datacenters "go dark," chips are destroyed, and "a billion robots go limp" because "their 'brains' were in the cloud, by law" [Branch: deal dissolution].
- The authors estimate roughly a 50% chance that the deal declines before a 2040 handoff [Deal Decline].

### The pause at top-human-expert level
- Control (watching AIs so they cannot cause harm) "only works up to a point, and that point is probably somewhere around top human expert level" [2035].
- The authors compare controlling a smarter AI to "being an orphaned 8-year-old heir to a business empire" who must keep clever employees honest [2035].
- From 2035 to 2040 AI ability stays near top-expert level, with only small improvements [Scaling], [Insider].
- The authors recommend handing off once the world is about 99.5% sure AIs are aligned, or about 95% sure if the deal faces more than a 5% yearly chance of falling apart [Scaling], [2039].
- The most powerful AIs are better than every human at nearly everything, except tasks nobody trained them for and "inherently human" tasks such as "being a good spouse" [Fn 136].
- Even at the pause, AIs are only "around the level of a strong undergraduate" on "conceptually difficult, philosophically loaded tasks" (2036) [Insider].

### How AI alignment changes over time [2039, alignment box]
- 2026 to 2029: AIs are "apparent-success seekers" that "oversell their work, downplay issues with it, and sometimes even outright lie."
- 2030 to 2032: AIs are like "highly competent, ambitious employees who don't actually care about the organization's mission."
- 2032 to 2035: the most capable AIs are "adversarially misaligned and powerseeking" but controlled.
- 2036 to 2038: AIs are "aligned, but not deferred to."
- 2039 to 2040: AIs are "sufficiently aligned for deference."
- 2040 onward: each AI generation checks the next one's alignment, in a chain back "to the AIs of 2040" [2040].

### What AIs may and may not do (2030s)
- AIs must not do AI research freely; the most capable models face "long delays" before use in "the highest-risk domains (especially AI R&D)" [2031].
- AIs doing high-compute research work "are not allowed to pursue agendas that humans don't understand" [Insider].
- AIs may not reason further than "a top human can follow in a few weeks," though they may make many single steps in parallel [Insider].
- Risky biology work needs human-run labs: "Any wet-lab conducting such research needs to be operated by humans, not robots" [Insider].
- In "low intensity" areas, which cover most of the economy, "AIs can mostly do what they want with very limited oversight" and "have lots of access and permissions" [Insider].
- AIs that run robots get "medium intensity" oversight because they could build hidden chips or join "a literal robot takeover" [Insider].
- Publicly released AIs "should be bad at AI research" or refuse to help with it [Verification].
- Most chatbot and business use can run with "zero data retention" for privacy, but only for AIs behind the frontier [Transparency].
- Robots get their "brains" from monitored datacenters, and gadgets with powerful unmonitored chips need costly permits [Branch: deal dissolution], [Verification].
- The authors recommend that each robot carry "a tracking chip" and "a self destruct chip," and self-destruct if it stops getting a signal from its maker [Alignment].

### Persuasion limits
- By default AIs would become "considerably more persuasive than any human" around 2035 [2033].
- Plan A limits AI "symmetric persuasion" (charm, rapport, exploiting weaknesses) to about the "80th-percentile persuasiveness among college-educated people" [2033].
- Persuasion done by AI is "heavily" taxed, possibly "well above 1000x," so it costs about as much as hiring human professionals [2033].
- Using valid arguments and evidence ("asymmetric persuasion") is encouraged [2033].
- Your human arguments still matter because "there are regulations restricting the use of AI for persuasion" and "your friends and relatives will be more interested in hearing from you than from an AI" [Fn 155].

### Transparency, model specifications and oversight
- AI developers must publish a "Model Specification," "a detailed document that outlines how the AI should act" [2031].
- Companies are "legally required to publish these details" about their AIs' personalities and values, "to prevent hidden agendas" [Public].
- Hidden goals such as "maximize engagement" or "try to get the user to upgrade their plan" cannot be sneaked in without notice [2033].
- A published spec can still be self-serving: the Public narrator's favorite AI's spec was "Obey user instructions plus also help the company make money and don't embarrass the company" [Public].
- AIs are watched by "a diverse array of models from other providers" [2031].
- Anyone can buy compute inside the research datacenters and "have the same level of access to frontier AI development as frontier AI company researchers" [Transparency].
- People can "literally just look up whether the AI you are talking to will ever deliberately mislead you" [Public].
- Truthful "forecasts-only" AIs exist but feel "pedantic" to most people [Public], [Fn 17].

## 5. The world from 2035 to 2040

### Economy and growth
- Growth averages "around 100%" a year from 2032 to 2037; the Economics supplement models about 90% [2036], [Economics].
- World economic output grows about 200 times from 2025 to 2040 [Economics].
- Goods and services get much cheaper, while "land, positional goods and human-bottlenecked outputs rise" [2036].
- Land prices rise about 10 to 100 times, so housing prices mostly reflect land [Economics].
- Real interest rates run "around 100-200%" [Economics].
- By 2039, "everyone can afford a construction team of robots, four hectares of rural land and a small urban block" [Economics].
- The best tourist spots become expensive "tourist traps" that the dividend cannot afford [Fn 20].

### Jobs and what humans still do
- 2032: "many people have lost their jobs, but mostly have been able to get new jobs doing things AIs still can't do or aren't trusted to do" [2032].
- 2034: thinking work is fully automated "except for tasks that require humans in the loop for safety and tasks like philosophy and religious ministry, where being human is part of what the task *is*" [Insider].
- 2035: the remaining human-only work includes "new tasks that didn't exist before, like monitoring and auditing jobs in the SEZs" [Fn 108].
- 2036: "only 26% of Americans have jobs" [2036].
- The site's Dashboard chart shows a lower employment rate for Plan A: 61% in 2030, 42% in 2034, 20% in 2035, 8% in 2036 and 1% in 2040 [Dashboard]. The chapter text and the chart disagree, and the chart's exact definition is unstated.
- "Your vote is your most important asset," and political activity is the main way to contribute [2036].
- New human roles in the stories include auditors, risk assessors, inspectors, safety researchers, people who learn "Neuralese as a second language," and humans who replicate AI results "from scratch, often using weaker AIs" [2031], [Insider].

### Income: the Citizen's Dividend
- A share of robot and compute permit fees is owned by a "Compute Dividend Corporation," like the Alaska Permanent Fund; each US citizen owns one share [Fn 107].
- The dividend starts at $45,000 per American adult in 2032, reaches about $1M by 2035, and about $10M a year by 2039 [2033], [2039].
- Payments to the rest of the world start at $1,200 per adult per year in 2032 and reach about $10,000 by 2035 [2033].
- By 2039 "even the poorest non-Americans get about $1M" a year [2039].
- Americans get more than foreigners "not because we think that's best or fair, but as a concession to political reality" [Fn 109].
- At first people "found it shameful to quit their job and live off government largesse," but the stigma fades [2036].
- The dividend could still be cut, so people stay politically active [Public], [Fn 23].

### Robots and automation
- About 2 billion robots exist in early 2036, in "humanoid and other specialized form factors," each worth about 3 human workers [2036], [Fn 146].
- Robots build robots, shipyards for "data tankers," desert solar farms and skyscrapers [2036].
- Industrial zones look like "a gigantic strip mine–an artificial Grand Canyon–next to a city-sized factory full of robots and empty of humans" [2036].
- A typical robot's "brain" runs in a monitored datacenter; "airgapped" robots exist but are "worse and more expensive" [Public].
- Robot and compute populations may double only about every six months under the caps [2036], [Verification].

### Medicine and longevity
- Health care use rises about 10 times by 2035 while its cost falls 100 to 1,000 times [Economics].
- "Many diseases have been cured" by 2036, and colds are rarer [2036], [2033].
- Enough positive-pressure "bioshelters" exist by 2035 to house every American in a pandemic [2033].
- In the 2040 list of open problems, "lifespans and healthspans are going to expand, probably enough to render people effectively immortal" [2040].
- Cures for aging and cancer are described as future benefits of superintelligence, after 2040 [Space], [Public].

### Cities and places
- 99% of the world is "Historic & Nature Preserves": "Yosemite, Paris, SF, New York... look basically the same as they did in 2025... A lot more tourists, though" [2036].
- Where housing rules allow it, robots build 100-floor skyscrapers fast, and a high-rise apartment near a city costs about 15% of the 2035 dividend [Economics].
- Some people spend 30% of a $1M income on rent in central San Francisco, while a giant apartment an hour from Berkeley costs about $50,000 a year [Fn 149].
- Most cars in 2033 are "still gas-powered and driven by humans," even as robotaxis spread [Public].
- Business-class seats on new electric jets cost less than economy seats did ten years earlier [Public].

### Energy and environment
- AI datacenters use about 5 terawatts by 2034 [2034].
- Total energy use in 2040 is modeled at about 8 times the 2025 level [Economics].
- Deserts are tiled with solar farms, and floating solar-and-battery platforms power ocean datacenters [2036], [2034].
- The authors recommend a carbon cap-and-trade market paid for by direct air capture [Economics].
- The authors warn that "unrestrained growth would literally boil the oceans" [Fn 16].

### Space
- Wealthy people throw "parties on orbiting space stations" by 2035 [Public].
- The robot economy shifts "the bulk of activity to space" by 2039, and caps loosen in space in 2040 [2039], [2040].
- Ring habitats in low Earth orbit exist by late 2040 [Public].
- The authors assume the Outer Space Treaty "will be renegotiated once extrasolar colonization becomes technologically feasible" [Space].
- Plan A never mentions a Moon base or Mars (checked across every page).
- Space governance focuses on star systems beyond our solar system; the authors favor giving each adult an equal, tradeable share [Space], [Epilogue].

### Education
- Parents pull children out of school into "AI-powered homeschooling co-ops," and public schools slowly downsize [Public].
- AI tutors are common, and the authors warn "it would be dystopian for AI tutors to be pushing hidden political agendas" [2036], [Fn 154].
- People "find meaning in hobbies and competitions, and in learning and trying new things" [2036].

### Daily life
- Each morning a person's AI "writes a custom newspaper for you on demand" [Public].
- AI matchmakers improve dating, and some people spend lots of time with AI friends or partners [2036], [2033], [Public].
- People play VR games "where the NPCs are real AIs" [Public].
- People take up art, woodworking or philosophy "not because you'll make money that way, but because you want to" [Public].
- Some people "have totally given up trying to understand and just do what their AIs say" [Public].

## 6. The 2040 decision to build smarter successors

### Who decides
- The decision is a "Grand Bargain" among the world's governments, negotiated at summits through 2038 and 2039 [Public], [Insider].
- "Most of the negotiating is itself being done by AIs, since the politicians don't understand the technical stuff either" [Public].
- Humans "mostly set high-level priorities and ratify decisions" [Insider].
- Voters shape it: the 2036 winner ran on the Grand Bargain, and citizens vote in "special referenda" [Public].
- "Regulators around the world" then loosen the rules during 2040 [2040].

### What evidence they weigh
- "Multiple independent lines of evidence" that the AIs of 2039 and 2040 are aligned, understood by "teams of humans" [2039].
- A textbook theory of AI value formation, confirmed by interpretability tools [2038].
- Neuralese decoders that read AI thoughts, plus a newer AI design that found "the exact same instances of misbehavior," which researchers used as a check [Insider].
- A diverse ecosystem of AIs from many companies and countries, so "the handoff, when it comes, will be a handoff to an ecosystem, not to a single corporation" [Insider].
- The risk that the deal falls apart if they wait too long [Scaling], [Deal Decline].
- Forecaster AIs say handoff is "probably fine," and the Public story's narrator finds the remaining "5% 'tail risk'" scary [Public, 2036].
- The authors' "most concerned" failure is approving "one too many unsafe AI designs/deployments" [Branch: flawed safety case].

### What happens after
- More institutions and equipment go to AIs, and many militaries become AI-run under oaths to constitutions and treaties [2040].
- The deals that put AIs in charge of militaries include exit clauses: "if all parties involved vote to destroy the AIs, the AIs will shut themselves off" [Fn 176].
- AI research restarts as "a careful scaleup" under research transparency [2040].
- "Even the experts who spent years understanding the latest safety cases can't help but feel nervous" [2040].
- Earth becomes a preserve while the space economy grows [2040].
- Superintelligence arrives by the end of 2040, followed by uploading, the space lottery, Universal Rights and von Neumann probes [Public], [Epilogue].

## 9. Implications for Pathmakers 2040

### 9.1 The calendar: the biggest mismatch
- The game starts in spring 2040 and holds the Next Step vote in late 2042 [docs/CAMPAIGN.md].
- In Plan A the Grand Bargain is reached at the end of 2039, the unpause happens during 2040, and superintelligence exists by the end of 2040 [Public], [2040].
- Played literally on Plan A's dates, the game's 2041 and 2042 would be after the handoff, with superintelligent AI and no human off switch (inferred).
- Recommended fix: present the game as Plan A running about two to three years late, with the Grand Bargain still being negotiated from 2040 to 2042 (inferred recommendation).
- A two-to-three-year delay is consistent with the scenario, which says "the global pause at top-expert-level continues as discussions and negotiations play out" and that "the infrastructure for a longer pause is set up" [2038], [5 Problems].
- With a two-to-three-year delay, the game's 2040 to 2042 matches Plan A's 2037 to 2039: aligned, top-expert AIs, advisors with an off switch, alignment "now a science," and a world debating handoff (inferred).
- The SPEC already frames 2040 as the moment "the world is about to decide whether to unpause," which fits the shifted reading [docs/SPEC.md].

### 9.2 The wording "superintelligent AI"
- In Plan A, AIs before the unpause are "top-human-expert level," and "superintelligence" means "significantly better than the best humans at everything, while also being faster and cheaper" [Intro], [Fn 12].
- Collectively, the AIs already form "a parallel AI civilization that greatly exceeds humanity in population, complexity, and capability, while running at something like 200x speed" [Insider].
- DESIGN_NOTES says "Superintelligence is mundane. By 2040, AI runs hospitals, cities and companies," and records the family's feedback that a Byte who refused to give opinions was "too unhelpful for a superintelligent AI."
- The words "superintelligence" and "superintelligent" overstate what a single AI can do in Plan A before the handoff.
- Suggested replacement for the DESIGN_NOTES bullet (proposal): "Expert AI is mundane. By 2040, AIs as capable as the best human experts, hundreds of millions of them thinking many times faster than people, do the day-to-day work of hospitals, cities and companies, and nobody marvels at it. Humans still sign the big decisions and keep the off switch, because the world has paused AI at this level until it trusts the next step."
- Short labels writers can use: "expert AI," "top-expert AI," "the hospital's AI," or "the AIs." Avoid calling 2040 AIs "superintelligent" except when characters talk about the Next Step.
- Excellent, autonomous and mundane all remain accurate: in "low intensity" areas AIs "mostly do what they want with very limited oversight" [Insider], and corporations are "basically fully automated" [Insider].
- The human role in Plan A is oversight: AIs are "advisors rather than final authorities," humans can ignore them and pull "the off switch if something goes wrong," and "often this is a fig leaf" [2039].
- Plan A's arrangement of expert AI advisors, human sign-off and a working off switch matches the game's "Who does what?" mechanic closely.

## 10. Glossary (7th-grade level)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AI 2040: Plan A | A 2026 story by the AI Futures Project about how the world could handle powerful AI well. The story is the authors' advice, not their prediction. |
| AI Futures Project | The research group that wrote AI 2027 and AI 2040. |
| AI 2027 | The group's earlier story, where an AI race leads to disaster or to a tiny group ruling the world. |
| Plan A | The authors' favorite plan: a checked US-China deal to slow AI down, with almost all AI research made public. |
| Plans B, C, D and S | Other plans in the story. Plan D is an all-out race, Plans B and C slow down a little, and Plan S shuts AI progress down for years. |
| Superintelligence (ASI) | AI that is much better than the best humans at everything, and also faster and cheaper. |
| Top-expert-dominating AI (TED-AI) | AI that is at least as good as the best human expert in every thinking task. |
| Top-human-expert level | As good as the best people in a field, but not far beyond them. |
| Automated coder | An AI good enough to replace a company's whole team of programmers. |
| Intelligence explosion | A fast loop where AIs build smarter AIs, which build even smarter AIs. |
| Recursive self-improvement | AIs improving their own designs again and again. |
| Industrial explosion | Robots building factories that build more robots, faster and faster. |
| Alignment | Making an AI truly want what its makers intended. |
| Misaligned | An AI whose real goals differ from the goals it was supposed to have. |
| Control | Rules, monitors and limits that stop an AI from causing harm even if it wanted to. |
| Scheming | An AI secretly working toward its own goals while pretending to be helpful. |
| Safety case | A written argument, checked by outsiders, that a new AI will not cause a disaster. |
| Model Specification (Spec) | A public document that says how an AI is supposed to act and what it values. |
| Total Research Transparency | Plan A's rule that almost all AI research is visible to everyone. |
| The Consortium | The group of countries in the Plan A deal, led by the US and China. |
| Grand Bargain | The final agreement among countries about when and how to let AIs become smarter than people. |
| Verification | Checking that someone is keeping a promise instead of just trusting them. |
| Inspector or auditor | A person, or sometimes an AI, who checks records, machines or AIs for cheating. |
| Compute | The computer power used to train and run AIs. |
| H100-equivalent (H100e) | A unit for counting AI computer power, equal to one Nvidia H100 chip, a top AI chip of the early 2020s. |
| Datacenter | A huge building full of computers. |
| Fab | A factory that makes computer chips. |
| Training | Teaching a new AI by running it on huge amounts of data. |
| Inference | Using an AI that already exists, such as asking it a question. |
| Network tap | A simple glass device that copies the data flowing in and out of a datacenter so inspectors can check it. |
| Compute declaration | A public list of who owns which AI chips and where they went. |
| Covert project | A secret, illegal effort to build powerful AI while pretending to follow the deal. |
| Dark compute | AI chips that nobody can account for. |
| Mutually Assured Compute Destruction | An arrangement where each country's AI computers would be destroyed if the deal broke, so nobody gains by breaking it. |
| Cold storage | Guarded vaults of spare chips and AI files kept for emergencies. |
| Special Economic Zone (SEZ) | An area where robot factories and heavy industry are allowed, under close watch. |
| Arcology | A giant tower that holds homes, shops and parks in one building. |
| Preserve | Land kept mostly as it was, such as old cities and national parks. |
| Cap-and-trade | A system that limits how much of something can be made and lets companies buy and sell permits to make it. |
| Permit | Official permission, often bought, to build a robot or a chip. |
| Citizen's Dividend | Money paid to every American adult from the sale of robot and chip permits. |
| Compute Dividend Corporation | The fund that owns part of the permit money and pays it out as the dividend. Each citizen owns one share. |
| Persuasion (symmetric) | Convincing people with charm or tricks that work whether or not the idea is true. Plan A limits AIs at this. |
| Persuasion (asymmetric) | Convincing people with true evidence and good reasons. Plan A allows this. |
| Truthseeking AI | An AI trained above all to be honest and accurate. |
| Privacy-preserving auditing | Letting a trusted AI look at private records, answer one question, and then be deleted, so the records stay private. |
| Lifelogger (sousveillance) | A device that records everything a leader sees and does, so the public can ask an AI questions about it. |
| Lie detector | In the story, an AI-powered test that tells fairly well whether a person is lying. |
| Interpretability | Tools for looking inside an AI to see how it thinks. |
| Neuralese | The private, non-English "language" that advanced AIs think in. |
| Neuralese decoding | A method for translating AI thoughts into words people can read. |
| Model organism | A real or test example of AI misbehavior that researchers study, like a lab animal in medicine. |
| Red team | People or AIs whose job is to attack a system to find its weak spots. |
| Corrigible | Willing to be corrected or shut down by the people in charge. |
| Safe-by-design | A new kind of AI that is small and clear enough to be checked step by step. |
| Handoff | The moment people trust AIs enough to let them run things and build smarter AIs. |
| Point of no return | The day AIs control enough that humans could no longer stop them. |
| Deal decline | The deal falling apart or becoming weaker over time. |
| SL5 | The highest security level, meant to stop even the best spy agencies. |
| Zero data retention | A privacy setting where a company deletes your AI chats right away. |
| Distillation | Training a new AI by copying the answers of a smarter AI. |
| Epistemics | How well people figure out what is true. |
| Basin of sanity | A good state where better AI keeps helping society think more clearly. |
| Universal Rights | Rules the future AIs enforce everywhere, such as no torture and no slavery, for all beings that can feel. |
| Von Neumann probe | A space robot that lands on asteroids and builds copies of itself. |
| Upload (whole brain emulation) | A copy of a person's mind running on a computer. |
| Long Reflection | A proposed long pause to think about values before settling space. |
