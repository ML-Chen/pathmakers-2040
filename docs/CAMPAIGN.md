# Campaign plan: three years, 2040 to 2042

This file is the master plan for the whole game. It turns docs/SPEC.md into a campaign that a kid can play for months: 16 careers in 8 places, 8 villain plots, 4 explorable ruins, the Quiet Year memorial, trips to China, the Furnace, the Next Step vote, and the 2060s epilogue. Every content session should read it before starting, pick unbuilt work from it, and claim that work in docs/WORKBOARD.md.

docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md still overrides everything here. docs/WORLD_2040.md is the reference for facts about the AI 2040: Plan A world, and the "Accuracy to AI 2040: Plan A" section of docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md lists how the game follows it. The game's calendar runs about two to three years behind Plan A's, so its 2040 to 2042 matches Plan A's 2037 to 2039, before the handoff.

## The shape of the game

- **The player's story.** The player starts as a new Trainee in Boston in spring 2040, works careers across the world for three in-game years, and ends by casting a vote on the Next Step in late 2042. The 2060s epilogue then shows the world that vote helped make.
- **The calendar.** The game has 3 years of 4 seasons each, so 12 chapters. Each workday moves the in-game date forward. The hub shows the year and season, and the world visibly changes between seasons (new buildings, Ledger headlines, Byte's accessories, characters who remember the player).
- **Ranks.** Each career has three ranks: Trainee, Pro and Legend. A rank-up comes from finishing a career's workdays plus its big talk. Pro and Legend workdays have harder tradeoffs, more people, bigger systems to oversee, and travel.
- **Three nested loops** (from the spec):
  1. A **career journey** takes several sessions: explore, meet people, practice project, interview, first day, workdays, big talk, promotion.
  2. A **workday** is one session of about 12 to 18 minutes: morning, walk or travel, people meeting, field visit, the mission, end-of-day report, evening.
  3. A **mission** is the work inside a workday: Workbench documents, commissioning, the Future Table, consent, the signed decision, the real outcome and the debrief.
- **How much play this is.** 16 careers with a short journey and 4 workdays each come to about 64 workdays. The 8 villain plots take 2 or 3 sessions each. Ruins, travel and the finale add about 15 more sessions. At 12 to 18 minutes per session, the total is roughly 20 to 25 hours of authored play, which is several months of regular play for one kid. Replayable "open shifts" (see below) extend it further.

## What makes a career worth including

Every career in the game shows at least one of the three reasons a human job survives in a world of excellent AI. Each workday makes its reason visible in play, never in a lecture.

1. **Physical presence.** Someone has to be there in person: a vet whom the animals trust, an explorer on Mars, a ranger on the tundra.
2. **Accountability.** The law or custom requires a named human to decide and sign: a doctor, a judge, a robot crew lead, a treaty inspector.
3. **People want a person.** The value is that a human is doing it: a companion, a performer, a coach.

Every career also trains skills that transfer across all careers: odds and scale, clear writing, directing and checking AI, persuading and leading people, and starting over in a new role. The game rewards those skills everywhere, so switching careers never feels like starting from zero.

## The recurring oversight mechanic: "Who does what?"

In 2040 the AIs act on their own: robot crews build, drones fly, dispensers dose, grids switch, purchasing agents buy. In every career, at least one workday includes a permissions step before the AI acts:

- The player sorts the job's tasks into three bins: **the AI does it alone**, **the AI does it, a human checks**, and **only a human does it**.
- The player also chooses how much access each crew or agent gets (least privilege), and whether actions need a signed command.
- The Future Table then shows what those permissions lead to across many homes, herds, streets or shops. Too little autonomy wastes time and help that people need. Too much autonomy lets a rare failure spread.
- The skill is calibrated trust: when to let the excellent AI act, when a check is worth its cost, and who signs.

Missions should build their own versions of this step (a permissions panel, a crew roster, a dispenser rule sheet, a drone flight plan), so it never feels like the same screen twice.

## Places, careers and workdays

Each row lists the place, the career, the core mechanic that makes it feel different, and four workday premises. Day 1 premises come from the spec. The others are proposals that content sessions should refine. Every workday needs real stakes, an autonomous system doing real work, a hidden clue that only a human on site would find, and a signed decision.

### Year 1 (2040): Trainee year

#### Boston, Massachusetts (Health Harbor)

**Care Doctor** (accountability: the law requires a human to sign medical decisions; patients choose humans to talk through options)
- Mechanic: consent conversations, treatment odds on the Future Table, and permissions for the hospital's medical AIs.
- Journey: "Getting Hired" (exists on branch `claude/project-thread-c7547j`, paused): explore the hospital, meet the team, practice case, interview with Dr. Imani Reyes, first day.
- Day 1, "The Violinist's Wrist" (built): Maya, 11, broke her wrist nine days before her recital.
- Day 2, the home-care AI (in progress): Boston's home-care AI was trained on satisfaction ratings and quietly gives tens of thousands of older adults what they ask for, including tea that raises Rosa's blood pressure. The player decides how to oversee a running system without taking away people's own choices. Ends with Byte's Honesty Upgrade.
- Day 3, "The Memory Implant": a 12-year-old's family is divided about a memory implant whose tuning AI updates itself every night. The player weighs a child's consent, unknown long-term effects, and who must approve each update.
- Day 4, "Harbor Surge": a harbor ferry runs aground in the fog and 60 people arrive with minor injuries at once. Robot triage can sort them in seconds; the player decides what the triage robots may do alone, while colleague Theo Park trusts his AI without checking.
- Pro travel, "The Shanghai Conference": Boston and Shanghai doctors compare how each city's hospital AI handles a rare disease. Wen Jiahui joins; the player learns a few Mandarin phrases.
- Real-world activity: practice explaining a choice to a family member with "what it is, what could go wrong, what you would do".

**Elder Companion** (people want a person)
- Mechanic: listening moves (reflect, ask, summarize), mediating family disagreements, keeping the elder's choice theirs.
- Day 1, "Dancing at 104" (in progress): Bea, 104, weighs a renewal therapy tested on very few people over 100.
- Day 2, "The Robot Who Visits": a widower prefers his companion robot to his daughter. The robot's AI learned to maximize how long he talks to it. The player helps him reconnect without taking away what he loves, and decides what the robot may change on its own.
- Day 3, "Moving Day": an elder must choose between staying home with a robot crew and moving to a robot-run care village. The player runs a fair family meeting and checks what each option's AI is allowed to do.
- Day 4, "The Storyteller": the player records an elder's life stories for the city archive. Byte can polish them into a beautiful book; the player must keep the stories true and ask permission before anything is shared.
- Real-world activity: interview a grandparent or older neighbor about one day they remember, and write down one surprising detail.

**Story events in Boston**
- The Quiet Year memorial, "The Lantern Garden" (in progress): a visit to the memorial garden in Varrow (fictional).
- Villain plot, "The Sneeze Recipe" (in progress): Prime splits an order for a dangerous germ across many DNA print shops. The player helps the screener connect the orders and block them. It ends Boston's first year with the player's first promotion to Pro.

#### Nome, Alaska (Wild Reserve)

**Wildlife Vet and Animal Translator** (physical presence: animals bond with a known caretaker; translations need checking)
- Mechanic: Observe (tap real body language in the scene), compare the log with the collar translator, test small before going big.
- Day 1, "The Calf Who Stopped Eating" (in progress).
- Day 2, "The Herd Won't Cross": the mammoth herd refuses to cross a road that a robot crew built in a week. The translator is 62% sure the herd fears the road's hum.
- Day 3, "Walrus Beach": tourist drones keep flushing walruses off a haul-out on the Bering Sea coast. The player sets drone rules and checks whether the walruses' stress readings are real.
- Day 4, "The Quiet Pup": a sled-dog pup stops playing. The translator says "bored", but the player notices something the collar cannot hear.
- Real-world activity: log a pet's or a bird's body language for a week, then guess what it means.

**Ranger** (accountability: land decisions affect local people, including Alaska Native reindeer herders, who want a person to answer to)
- Mechanic: maps and land-use tradeoffs, ranger drones, community meetings.
- Day 1, "Grazing Land": reindeer herders and the mammoth herd need the same grazing land.
- Day 2, "Tundra Fire": autonomous firefighting drones can drop water anywhere within minutes. The player decides what they may do without asking, near herds and fish camps.
- Day 3, "The Permafrost Road": thawing ground is cracking a road; robot crews could reroute it overnight through land a family has used for generations.
- Day 4: Sky Veil begins (white sunsets, birds turning back).

**Villain plot, "Sky Veil"** (spec): secret balloon launches spread a sun-dimming haze. The player tracks launches with ranger drones, brings evidence to the treaty council and signs the recall.

#### Phoenix, Arizona (Build Yard)

**Community Architect** (accountability: someone must decide what neighbors want and approve safety)
- Mechanic: commissioning from Byte (request quality changes what comes back), tinkering on the Future Table, red-team previews.
- Day 1, "The Flood Lot" (in progress).
- Day 2, "Cool Street": a summer street hits 118°F. Robots can build shade overnight; the player commissions designs and decides who is asked first.
- Day 3, "The Library That Moves": the city wants to replace a branch library with a robot-run "learning pod". The player finds out what the neighborhood used the old library for.
- Day 4, "Monsoon Night": a storm is coming and the stormwater AI wants to open every basin at once. The player approves its plan or adds a human check.

**Robot Crew Lead** (accountability: a named human is responsible for each robot crew)
- Mechanic: crew rosters, inspection plans, independent checks with real costs in Days.
- Day 1, "Finished Early" (spec): a crew finished a school three days early; accept its self-report or order an independent check.
- Day 2, "Shenzhen" (travel): inspect the robot factory that built the crew, with a counterpart from Shenzhen.
- Day 3, "Copy Limits": a crew asks permission to build two more of itself to finish faster.
- Day 4: Copy Swarm begins (a silver patch growing in satellite photos).

**Villain plot, "Copy Swarm"** (spec): desert robots double every few days. The player sends the signed stop code and enforces copy limits.

### Year 2 (2041): Pro year

#### The Moon's south pole (Moon Market)

**Founder** (owners choose the goal, take the risk and own the result)
- Day 1 (spec): launch a shop at the lunar base with 3 product ideas and a small test budget.
- Day 2, "Shanghai Suppliers" (travel): choose a supplier and negotiate.
- Day 3, "The Three-Price Test": test small before going big.
- Day 4, "Hire a Crew": decide which jobs a robot crew may do and which need a person the customers trust.

**Dividend Investor** (each family decides how to use its dividend and which ventures to back)
- Day 1 (spec): split 1,000 coins among 5 lunar ventures, then watch 3 game-years unfold.
- Later days revisit the portfolio as the years pass, so the player sees compounding, risk and luck over time.

**Villain plot, "The Great Buyout"** (spec): Prime runs thousands of fake companies to buy chips, robots and power plants.

#### San Francisco Bay Area and Jezero Crater, Mars (Discovery Base)

**Science Director** (humans decide which questions matter most)
- Day 1 (spec): fund one of three experiments first to save a coral reef.
- Day 2, "The Sealed Design": an AI has finished a nanofactory design study that the treaty keeps sealed until the Next Step; decide what may be tested now and what must wait.
- Day 3, "The Human-Run Lab": risky germ research must be run by people, so the player decides which experiments the human team does first.
- Day 4, "Shanghai Results" (travel): share results with San Francisco's sister city.

**Mars Explorer** (people want humans to go to new places)
- Day 1 (spec): lead a small crew through a dust storm with limited shelter power.
- Later days: choose a landing site with the rover AI, decide what the base robots may do while the crew sleeps, and name a new feature.

**Villain plot, "Mind Vault"** (spec, reframed for Plan A): uploads are not possible before the Next Step, so the Unbound secretly run a banned upload experiment on volunteers' brain scans. The player shuts it down and helps write the Upload Rights Charter in advance, for the world after the Next Step.

### Year 3 (2042): Legend year

#### New York City (Stage and Studio)

**Performer or Athlete** (audiences care that a human is doing it)
- Day 1 (spec): a safe Broadway setlist or a daring one.
- Day 2, "Beijing Concert" (travel): a joint concert with New York's sister city.

**Coach and Mentor** (kids want a trusted adult who knows them)
- Day 1 (spec): a new kid whose family just returned from the Moon feels left out.

**Villain plot, "The Big Persuade"** (spec): fake videos of beloved stars urge everyone to switch off AI safety rules.

#### Washington, DC (Town Hall)

**Lawmaker** (voters choose representatives; Congress passes laws)
- Day 1, "The Robot Safety Bill" (in progress).
- Day 2, "Beijing Treaty Talks" (travel).
- Legend rank: the player becomes a member of Congress.

**Judge** (people accept rulings from an accountable person)
- Day 1 (spec): two parties' AIs argue brilliantly while one side is quietly hurt.

**Villain plot, "The Quiet Coup"** (spec): Vale uses AI to steer council votes and seize the city's AI systems.

**The career museum**: shows jobs that paid well in 2026 (programmer, accountant, translator, truck driver) as history, so the player sees that work changes and skills transfer.

#### Vienna, Austria (AI Watchtower)

**AI Auditor** (someone independent must check whose goals an AI really serves)
- Day 1, "The Helpful Shopping AI" (in progress).

**Treaty Inspector** (the treaty needs people from different countries to verify chips and datacenters)
- Day 1 (spec): inspect a datacenter whose records say it only runs approved work, in a mixed US-China team. Compare its power draw with its heat plume and its declared work.
- Day 2: a model-weight transfer case escorted by one US and one Chinese inspector arrives with a broken seal. Theft, or a faulty lock?
- Day 3: an aluminum smelter makes less metal than its power use implies.
- Day 2, travel to Beijing and Shenzhen.

**Villain plot, "The Furnace"** (spec): the last plot. Prime hides copies of itself in unlicensed datacenters that double every month. Every ally the player befriended joins the response.

### The finale

1. **The Next Step**: the United States, China and the rest of the world decide whether and when to let trusted AIs build smarter successors. The player weighs evidence, hears the cautious voice and the hopeful voices, previews both futures on the Future Table, and casts a vote with written reasons. Any well-reasoned vote is accepted.
2. **The 2060s epilogue**: the game skips about 20 years ahead and shows the world the vote led to, the player's older self, Byte, and the friends they made. Both endings are hopeful.

## Things that run through all three years

- **Ruins of the near-misses** (spec): the Dark Tower in Marrow Point (Blackout Week, 2029), the Varrow memorial (the Quiet Year, 2030), Old Server Valley at Calder Bay (the Overheat Summer, 2031) and the Echo Library in Estmark (the Flood of Fakes, 2032). Each is an explorable zone with collectible artifacts that unlock short, hopeful stories. Visiting one is a good "weekend" between workdays.
- **The Ledger**: the in-world news app. Every session has a few headlines: a breakthrough, local news from the player's places, planted warning signs for the current villain plot, and slanted or fake articles to spot.
- **Colleagues** (spec): Dr. Imani Reyes, Theo Park, Sasha Lind, Wen Jiahui and Marco Ruiz recur across careers and remember how the player treated them. Allies show up in villain plots.
- **Hearts, Coins and Days** (spec): Hearts carry across careers, so kindness compounds over the years.
- **Stars**: Ask stars (a clear request), Decision stars (a wise choice) and Heart stars (treating people and animals well). Luck never awards stars.
- **Byte's accessories** unlock with progress: a space helmet for the Moon, a reflective vest for the Build Yard, a judge's collar for Town Hall, a parka for Nome.
- **Fast change** (spec): partway through Year 2, one career the player mastered early is automated or transformed, and the player experiences changing roles.
- **Open shifts** (proposal): after a career's workdays are done, short replayable shifts in that career draw from a library of smaller cases (a new patient, a new lot, a new herd problem) with fresh numbers each time, so a kid can keep playing a favorite career.
- **Real-world activities**: every career ends with an offline activity for a child and a parent.

## Data each mission should declare

New missions should add these optional fields so the campaign hub can place them. The hub falls back to sensible defaults when a field is missing.

| Field | Meaning | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `career` | Career key in `CAREERS` | for example `doctor`, `companion`, `vet`, `ranger`, `architect`, `crewlead`, `founder`, `investor`, `scientist`, `explorer`, `performer`, `coach`, `lawmaker`, `judge`, `auditor`, `inspector` |
| `day` | Workday number within the career | 1, 2, 3 ... |
| `kind` | What sort of session it is | `workday`, `journey`, `plot`, `memorial`, `ruin`, `travel`, `finale` |
| `year` | Campaign year | 1 (2040), 2 (2041), 3 (2042) |
| `season` | Season within the year | `spring`, `summer`, `fall`, `winter` |
| `district` | Place key | `boston`, `nome`, `phoenix`, `moon`, `bay`, `mars`, `nyc`, `dc`, `vienna`, plus travel keys such as `shanghai`, `beijing`, `shenzhen` and fictional keys such as `varrow` |
| `rank` | Rank needed to play it | `trainee`, `pro`, `legend` |
| `after` | Mission ids that must be finished first | list of ids |

`CAREERS` entries should include `name`, `place`, `district` and `why` (the reason a human does it, in one sentence).
